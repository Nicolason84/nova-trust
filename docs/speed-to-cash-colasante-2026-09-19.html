The delivery gap / A constructive response to Luis Colasante

Power, on time.
Cash, in time.

A megawatt can be reserved long before it earns a euro. Explore the distance between an energised site and a financeable cash flow.

Colasante’s central point holds: delivery and capital must share a workable timetable. The next step is to test the dependencies—not just list the milestones.

One useful refinement: physical load, contractual billing and collected cash are different clocks. A capacity contract can decouple them. It cannot make an unfinished facility deliverable.

02 / Evidence, not atmosphere

The bottleneck is real.
The inference still needs care.

Separate a published observation from what it can—and cannot—tell an investment committee.

IEA / 2025 ASSESSMENT~20%

Potential delay exposure

The IEA estimated that around one fifth of planned data-centre projects could face delays unless grid risks were addressed. This is a conditional sector estimate, not a default probability for a specific site. [01]

RTE / MAY 2026 SNAPSHOT~18 GW

Reserved, not all energised

RTE reported reserved capacity for about 80 projects, versus around 5 GW at end-2024. A reservation is not evidence of completed works, immediate demand or cash collection. [02]

RTE / OBSERVED RAMP~20%

Power draw is not rent

Sites connected to RTE for two to three years were using, on average, about 20% of requested power. That is an electrical-load observation—not a rental occupancy, billing or profitability measure. [02]

What the post gets right

Its strongest contribution is the timing mismatch: construction spending, delivery obligations, revenue and debt can move on different schedules. The IEA’s 2026 update also emphasises bottlenecks across electricity, equipment, chips and financing—not power alone. [03]

What should not be generalised

RTE describes power demand ramping over many years; this is not a rent-commencement forecast. Our modelling inference is to inspect the lease before converting utilisation into revenue. Nor is every data centre economically equivalent to a regulated utility. [02]

04 / Constructive underwriting

Don’t just price the delay.
Design who can absorb it.

Proposed diligence and structuring options. Their value and availability depend on project evidence, counterparty strength and negotiated terms.

01

Turn MW into deliverable milestones

Record the exact connection offer, firmness conditions, network works, permits, equipment slots, owner and acceptance test. Map dependencies rather than treating a PPA or capacity reservation as energisation.

Evidence to request: executed connection terms, works programme and independent engineering review. A commercial energy agreement alone does not establish site deliverability.

02

Separate demand from payment

Inspect commencement conditions, minimum capacity charges, service levels, collection terms and termination rights. Model a contracted floor only where a creditworthy counterparty is actually bound to pay.

Trade-off: minimum payments may improve cash visibility but affect pricing, tenant acceptance and concentration risk. They do not remove delivery conditions or default risk.

03

Fund the trough, not the average

Match construction drawdowns to verified progress. Test a funded reserve against the monthly cash low point; assess repayment grace and maturity separately. Price an extension rather than assuming it exists.

Trade-off: more equity consumes capital; longer tenor and standby facilities can cost more. They buy resilience, not automatic project value.

Additional options—and the cost they must carry

Phased commissioning: earlier rentable blocks may improve timing, but only if power, permits, cooling, fire safety and tenant acceptance support independent operation. Shared infrastructure and smaller phases may raise unit costs.

Flexible loads or temporary onsite generation: assess reliability, permitted operating hours, emissions, fuel and equipment availability. The IEA’s 2026 analysis warns that onsite gas generation is not necessarily the faster route at scale. [03]

Separate the assets: land, electrical and cooling infrastructure, servers and the AI service business do not carry identical technology, contract or residual-value risks. Allocate diligence and financing to the layer being funded.

One decision record per gate: dated evidence → accountable owner → unresolved dependency → consequence in months and cash → specific action. Do not replace missing documents with an invented confidence percentage.

05 / Reproducible, bounded method

Keep the uncertainty.

What this model actually does

Billed revenue = billed IT MW × 1,000 × €/kW/month
Receipts[m] = invoices[m − collection lag]
CFADS proxy = receipts − cash operating costs
− maintenance − explicit cash taxes
Interest = (opening debt + half-month draw) × rate/12
Cash[m] = cash[m−1] + CFADS + debt draw + equity draw
− CAPEX − interest − scheduled principal
Operational DSCR = rolling 12-month CFADS
/ rolling 12-month regular service
Maturity gap = max(0, final debt − notional cash)

Conventions and limits

Financial close is M0. Base building completion and power delivery are M24; commissioning takes two months. First invoicing follows completion of the relevant gates, in the next monthly period. Base customer readiness is M26. Usage increases linearly across the ramp period.

Base CAPEX is funded by committed debt and construction equity, drawn evenly over actual construction months. Overruns do not enlarge the debt commitment or construction equity: they consume the opening cash reserve. Interest uses average monthly drawn debt. Scheduled principal starts on the selected date; remaining debt is due at maturity.

The reserve is prefunded additional equity, not free capital. Default cash tax is explicitly zero; change it for scenario analysis. Electricity pass-through is excluded from revenue. Variable cash costs follow receipts as a simplifying timing assumption. Maintenance is a user-set proxy, not a measured requirement.

Not included: a full tax model, VAT, tenant default, bad debts, contractual penalties, reserve restrictions, intra-month payment ordering, detailed working capital, inflation, fees, exit value, refinancing execution, IRR or NPV. The DSCR shown is an analytical proxy—not the definition or test date in a loan agreement. It excludes the final balloon, which is displayed separately.

If cash becomes negative, later rows are a counterfactual funding ledger. No emergency borrowing is silently added. The maturity gap includes any still-unfunded deficits. Minimum opening reserve and exports can be reproduced without an API. No automatic assertion of commercial viability is made.
